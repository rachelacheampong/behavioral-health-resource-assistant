# Problem Statement

## Background

Behavioral health professionals frequently help clients navigate a wide range of community resources, including mental health services, substance use treatment, crisis intervention, housing support, food assistance, and other social services. These resources are often maintained across multiple reference guides and require staff to quickly identify options that best align with a client's unique circumstances.

While comprehensive resource guides are valuable, locating the most appropriate services can be time-consuming, particularly for new staff, during high-demand periods, or when a client presents with multiple needs. Staff may also spend additional time comparing providers, identifying eligibility requirements, and documenting referrals.

## The Problem

The current referral process relies heavily on manual searches and staff familiarity with available resources. As the number of community resources grows, navigating multiple documents and identifying appropriate referrals becomes increasingly difficult. This can reduce efficiency, create inconsistencies in referrals, and increase administrative workload.

The challenge is not a lack of available resources. Instead, it is the time and effort required to organize existing information into clear, actionable recommendations while maintaining consistency across staff.

## Proposed Solution

The Behavioral Health Resource Assistant explores how Claude can function as a decision-support tool that assists behavioral health professionals during the referral process.

Rather than replacing professional judgment, the assistant helps staff by:

- Interpreting natural language requests
- Identifying relevant community resources based on client needs
- Explaining why recommended resources may be appropriate
- Asking clarifying questions when necessary
- Generating concise referral summaries for documentation

The assistant is designed to organize existing information more efficiently while keeping behavioral health professionals responsible for all final decisions.

## Intended Users

This project is designed for professionals who regularly connect individuals to behavioral health and community resources, including:

- Linkage to Care staff
- Care coordinators
- Case managers
- Community health workers 
- Public health professionals
- Behavioral health navigators

## Expected Benefits

If implemented successfully, this approach could:

- Reduce the time required to locate appropriate resources
- Improve consistency in referrals across staff
- Support newer employees with resource navigation
- Improve documentation quality through standardized referral summaries
- Allow staff to spend more time interacting with clients rather than searching resource guides

## Scope

This project demonstrates how a large language model can support behavioral health resource navigation using structured reference materials.

The assistant is **not** intended to:

- Diagnose behavioral health conditions
- Provide medical or clinical advice
- Replace licensed professionals
- Determine emergency response needs
- Guarantee resource availability or eligibility
- Make final referral decisions

Human review and professional judgment remain essential throughout the referral process.
